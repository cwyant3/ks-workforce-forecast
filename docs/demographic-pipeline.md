# Resident workforce pipeline: 18-year demographic outlook

The demographic view replaces the ten-year LAUS trend projection in Workforce Outlook, County Brief and Full Explorer. The prior statistical module remains available for historical tests but is not rendered by those views.

The new view follows estimated resident cohorts from reference year 2024 through 2042. It models civilian labor-force participants **age 18+**, including older workers, rather than projecting historical labor-force growth. Headline LAUS observations remain age 16+ and are not spliced into this model. An 18-year horizon from today would require an updated or bridged population baseline; this version explicitly uses eighteen years from the available ACS reference year.

## Inputs and construction

- [Census ACS 2024 five-year B01001](https://api.census.gov/data/2024/acs/acs5/groups/B01001.html): all 105 Kansas county age-sex distributions, 2020–2024 period. Allocate ages evenly within each published band below 85. Allocate the open 85+ band using sex-specific national life-table survivors through age 119. These are modeled single ages, not observed single-age county counts.
- [Census ACS 2024 five-year B23001](https://api.census.gov/data/2024/acs/acs5/groups/B23001.html): civilian labor-force counts divided by total residents within each age-sex band. This effective resident share carries baseline exclusions into the resident-population model; it is not the official civilian noninstitutional LFPR denominator. Use the 16–19 share for modeled ages 18–19. A zero resident denominator uses the corresponding Kansas age-sex share and is prominently disclosed. Negative/suppressed source estimates fail validation rather than becoming zeros.
- [SSA 2023 period life table](https://www.ssa.gov/oact/STATS/table4c6.html), used in the 2026 Trustees Report: sex-specific annual death probabilities held constant. These national rates are an explicit modeling assumption, not county-specific mortality projections.

For each year, multiply each sex/single-age cell by its survival probability, shift it forward exactly one year, and apply the participation share for the attained age. No automatic retirement at 65. The published table ends at 119 with positive survival. Terminal survivors remain in an open 119+ cell using age-119 mortality; the shift never discards them. The baseline assumes zero net migration. No new births are needed for an age-18+ outlook over eighteen years: those born after the baseline do not attain age 18 within the horizon. The 2020–2024 ACS multiyear distribution is treated as a synthetic 2024 baseline, not a precise census taken on that date.

The structure follows [Census cohort-component methods](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/popproj/about.html) and the population-times-participation logic in [BLS projection methods](https://www.bls.gov/emp/documentation/projections-methods.htm). This limited cohort-component baseline is not an official Census/BLS county projection or a complete forecast of future county population.

## Presentation and scenarios

The main chart is a population-weighted violin view with a 2024–2042 year slider. It shows tracked residents in age ranges 0–17, 18–24, 25–54, 55–64 and 65+. Vertical position is age; width is resident density per year of age. One common width scale is computed across all years and enabled scenario paths. Gaussian smoothing uses a fixed 1.25-year bandwidth, with kernels renormalized within each band. It operates on aggregate weights, never fabricated person-level records. Dots show weighted medians and bars show the interquartile age range. Labels give modeled band counts; empty bands retain a zero label without a violin. The y-axis is fixed at 0–120, with the terminal modeled age representing 119+.

The youngest group includes new birth cohorts. Migration scenarios affect age distributions, while participation scenarios affect only labor-force totals. A selected-year caption separates modeled all-age residents from age-18+ civilian labor-force estimates. The prior stacked labor-force trend is retained inside a detail expander. Companion bars show residents reaching 18 and 65 each year under the baseline. These are potential entry and aging indicators, not actual hires, retirements or openings. Enrollment is not added to population. County school/home-school counts are not integrated.

Optional scenario controls specify annual net migration of age-18–64 residents as a percentage of baseline age-18–64 residents, and a change in each adult age-sex civilian labor-force share by 2042. The migration amount is constant in persons each year. Arrivals use the baseline working-age distribution; departures use the current working-age distribution, capped at available residents. Applied migration is exported and any cap triggers a warning. New migrants then age and survive normally. Participation changes phase in linearly, bounded to 0–100%. The orange line shows the selected scenario; it does not alter other dashboard models.

The violin shapes describe resident ages, not uncertainty bands. No 80% coverage claim is made. ACS sampling error, future mortality improvements, age-specific migration behavior and changing participation are not assigned probabilities. Scenarios demonstrate specified assumptions rather than establishing likelihoods.

## Reproduction and provenance

### Current birth model: age-specific fertility

The Census-style county adaptation replaces the direct birth-count forecast. It computes births as the sum of projected female exposure at each modeled maternal age multiplied by that age's rate. It is not an official Census county forecast.

1. **Local calibration:** [KDHE KIC](https://kic.kdhe.ks.gov/birth.php) resident live births pooled across calendar years 2020–2024, divided by five times ACS 2024 five-year female population. These are approximate matched-period woman-years. Native maternal groups are 10–14, 15–17, 18–19, 20–24, 25–29, 30–34 and 35+. Within groups, Census's age pattern allocates rates to modeled single ages. The under-15 tail is placed at age 14; 35+ is modeled as 35–54. Neither allocation is observed county single-age fertility. Each local rate is stabilized using 1,000 woman-years at the corresponding Kansas rate, an explicit assumption. Suppressed cells and zero female exposures use a disclosed state proxy without reconstructing suppressed counts. Kansas female exposure is the sum of 105 county ACS estimates. Eighty counties require a proxy in at least one maternal band. Unknown maternal ages are excluded from rate calibration.
2. **Future rates:** [Census NP2023 A1](https://www.census.gov/data/datasets/2023/demo/popproj/2023-popproj.html), GROUP0, supplies single-age fertility schedules 14–54 through 2100. Units are births per woman-year. The mean of the 2024/2025 year-ended schedules supplies a calendar 2024 age-pattern proxy. Each future annual rate follows the corresponding national relative change applied to the local calibrated rate. This transfers a national assumption; it does not claim county-specific future fertility was estimated. Zero-reference ages remain zero when future rates are also zero; a newly positive rate fails validation instead of dividing by zero.
3. **Timing/level bridge:** Local calibration uses calendar births, while Census component history and future schedules use July–June intervals. The ACS period estimate is treated as a synthetic July 2024 population. A county-wide scalar aligns the model's 2025 births to the published Census 2025 estimate. All paths use that observed estimate; projections begin 2026. This level bridge is calibration, not independent validation or a point-in-time population reconciliation.
4. **Paths:** Central uses the calibrated Census-relative age schedules. Low/high multipliers phase from 1 in 2025 to 0.8/1.2 in 2042. They are analyst sensitivity assumptions, not official Census fertility variants or probability bounds. Birth counts need not differ by exactly 20 percent because female newborn cohorts can themselves contribute births toward the horizon's end.

Each annual step uses mean start/end female exposure after aging and survival. Adult migrants arrive at year-end, so they affect births starting the following year. Newborn cohorts enter with half-year survival and use later midpoint-age transitions; this avoids repeated full infant mortality. Their sex distribution uses the baseline under-five mix. Ages 14–17 of future female birth cohorts can contribute further births. Those cohorts remain under 18 through 2042, so fertility choices do not change the age 18+ workforce through this endpoint. Fertility does not depend on participation settings. Child migration and migrant-specific fertility differences remain outside this model.

The main Low/Central/High control updates the violin and selected-year births. All three fertility paths, plus enabled migration alternatives, share the same violin width scale. The details show annual birth trajectories, maternal-age schedules, female exposure, proxy bands, calibration factor, historical comparisons and downloads with county/path identities.

### Historical comparison and limits

The chart retains 20 annual Census birth observations 2006–2025, with source-vintage transitions 2011/2021. Separate KDHE calendar births 2006–2024 and ACS native female-age estimates 2010–2024 support a retrospective cohort-mechanism diagnostic. Each origin 2015–2023 uses only its own and prior observations: pooled five-year age rates are frozen and female cohorts age forward with no mortality or migration. Uniform within-band age schedules, particularly the 35+ tail, differ from the production model. Five simple birth-count benchmarks use the same eligible 1-, 3- and 5-year outcomes. Independently published all-age county totals supply outcomes; suppressed maternal-age cells remain hidden.

This is **not an exact backtest of the production Census-relative schedule**: applying a 2023 projection to earlier origins would leak future information. ACS rolling five-year exposures are approximate, overlap across vintages, and were retrieved retrospectively. Rates omit unknown maternal ages while targets include them. Comparison errors are not independent model-selection validation or 18-year accuracy evidence. Sedgwick has 21 matched comparisons per model: frozen-cohort MAE 493.65 births, versus 186.17 for the best count benchmark (five-year damped trend). These results do not establish improved forecast accuracy; they make the demographic mechanism and its limitations reviewable.

For Sedgwick's no-net-migration baseline, 2025 births are 6,645. The 2042 paths are approximately 5,160 Low, 6,451 Central and 7,742 High. The central decline is generated by maternal-age structure and fertility schedules, not a forced slope. The 2025 level factor is 1.01794. All 105 counties across three fertility paths are finite/nonnegative, ordered and preserve age-18+ workforce totals. All 171 automated tests passed at the 2026-10-21 milestone (the full suite is 180 tests as of 2026-11-08). Every county has 21 matched historical comparisons for each of six methods, with no skipped origins or targets. Runtime checks cover all three fertility choices, the 2042 slider, migration, proxy counties and all three affected dashboard views.

Reproduction: `scripts/build_birth_inputs.py` retains Census 20-year history; `scripts/build_fertility_inputs.py --cache-only` rebuilds KDHE sources; `scripts/build_female_history.py --normalize-only` rebuilds historical female exposure; `scripts/build_census_fertility_inputs.py` rebuilds projected rate schedules. Source manifests retain original bytes, hashes, query parameters and period/suppression definitions. Current model: `fertility_model.py`; retrospective diagnostic: `fertility_validation.py`. Earlier `birth_forecast.py` remains as a comparison benchmark, not the violin's active birth model.

Source builder: `scripts/build_demographic_inputs.py`; source bundle: `data/demographic_inputs/`. Original Census JSON and official group labels are retained. Source manifest documents URLs, acquisition, dates and hashes; survival acquisition limitations are explicit. Runtime reads only local files and performs no network requests. Existing adopted release tables and stored forecasts are unchanged.
